THE FLAGSHIP PRODUCT

Made tangible.
Made to understand.

The TEL Model Rocket Kit connects a physical model with a guided, institution-led learning experience.

More than a box of parts.

The learning product brings the model, preparation and classroom reflection together. An educator decides how the experience connects with the students’ age, topic and school environment.

01 / MODEL

Something to see and discuss.

A model rocket, its main components and recovery elements provide a reference for systems and design conversations.

02 / PREPARATION

Responsibility belongs in the lesson.

Protective accessories and a teacher-led preparation pathway support the agreed programme. Final contents are confirmed for each proposal.

03 / LEARNING

Give the moment a purpose.

Prediction, observation and reflection connect the experience with physics and give students a useful basis for discussion.

FIND YOUR PLACE

Five ways TEL can
enter your school.

Explore a format below. Each opens here, with the learning focus, activities and school planning in one place.

01Curriculum connections+

WITHIN EXISTING LESSONS

Put physics in context.

Use the kit as a shared reference for concepts students already meet in physics.

Where it fits

A physics lesson or a short sequence of lessons, especially around Classes X–XI topics.

What students can do

  • Start with a prediction about forces or motion.
  • Connect an observed outcome to the relevant concept.
  • Use measurements or a sketch for reflection, with the educator choosing the level.

What the school plans

Select the class, topic and learning aim. Nominate an educator to decide what is appropriate for the students.

A useful takeaway

A concept explanation, an observation sheet or a short class discussion. This is enrichment connected to curriculum topics, not a separate qualification.

Discuss this format with TEL
02ATL & innovation lab+

ALONGSIDE YOUR EXISTING LAB

Build a systems mindset.

Bring an aerospace-themed project into the practical learning already happening in your school.

Where it fits

An ATL, innovation room or supervised school project group.

What students can do

  • Identify the parts of a system and their purpose.
  • Discuss design choices, material choices and constraints.
  • Document observations and explain a proposed improvement.

What the school plans

Align the activity with lab rules, available space and a supervising educator. Practical activity needs a separately agreed scope and permissions.

A useful takeaway

A design journal, a labelled system diagram or a group presentation.

Discuss this format with TEL
03STEM & science club+

A FOCUSED ENRICHMENT MODULE

Give curiosity a regular home.

Give a motivated student group a defined project without changing the formal timetable.

Where it fits

A science club, STEM club or a teacher-supported enrichment group.

What students can do

  • Divide research, observation and documentation roles.
  • Share questions and investigate them with educator guidance.
  • Present what the group learned, including what did not go as expected.

What the school plans

Choose the group, club timetable and adult lead. Agree the format and responsibilities with TEL before planning a practical session.

A useful takeaway

A club showcase, a reflective article or a peer explanation.

Discuss this format with TEL
04Project week & showcase+

AN OCCASION WITH A CLEAR PURPOSE

Make the learning visible.

Create a structured story of prediction, preparation, observation and reflection around a school occasion.

Where it fits

A project week, science programme or a supervised STEM showcase.

What students can do

  • Plan the learning question and the student presentation.
  • Keep an observation and documentation record.
  • Explain the process and learning to the wider school community.

What the school plans

Agree the date, audience, venue and staff responsibilities. A showcase does not remove practical permissions or supervision requirements.

A useful takeaway

A student presentation or display showing the questions, observations and learning outcomes.

Discuss this format with TEL
05Teacher-led demonstration+

AN EDUCATOR-LED FORMAT

Let observation lead the lesson.

Let an institution-appointed adult lead the practical component while students focus on concepts and questions.

Where it fits

A school that prefers a demonstration-led learning format.

What students can do

  • Predict an outcome before observing.
  • Discuss roles, boundaries and responsible decisions.
  • Reflect on the observation using age-appropriate physics.

What the school plans

Appoint the adult lead and confirm the environment and permissions. SafeFlight supports preparation; it cannot authorise a practical activity.

A useful takeaway

An observation record, a guided worksheet or a class discussion.

Discuss this format with TEL

PLAN IT TOGETHER

From first enquiry
to a school proposal.

The school and TEL agree the educational format, delivery responsibilities and commercial scope before a programme is confirmed.

  1. Share the learning context.

    Your school, location, class group, learning goal and preferred period are enough for a first conversation. Do not send pupil lists.

  2. Choose a suitable format.

    Discuss the curriculum, lab, club, project week or educator-led route, and how it fits the existing timetable.

  3. Review the proposal.

    Agree the scope, contents, adult lead, venue requirements, permissions, delivery plan and commercial terms.

  4. Prepare and reflect.

    Use the agreed learning pathway, then review the students’ observations and the learning outcomes with the educator.

BEFORE WE TALK

Useful answers.
A clear next step.

School enquiries are open for 2027. Public individual product availability remains coming soon.

Can TEL fit into a physics lesson?

Yes, the proposal can focus on topics such as forces, motion, energy or measurement. The educator selects the appropriate level and learning objective; this is not an NCERT endorsement or a new qualification.

Do we need to create a new subject?

No. The five formats are designed around existing lessons, labs, clubs and school occasions. The final format is agreed with your institution.

Does SafeFlight approve a launch?

No. The public version is a learning demonstration. A school-appointed adult retains practical control, and real activity needs the appropriate permissions, environment and agreed responsibilities.

Can individuals order the kit now?

Individual public availability is coming soon. The current route is a school or institutional enquiry, not an online checkout or a paid reservation.

What should we include in a first enquiry?

A school name, city, adult contact, broad class group, preferred period and learning goal. Do not send students’ personal information through the general enquiry form.
